# Quality checklist

Use this checklist as a final pass. Mark an item as not applicable when the
medium or document mode makes it irrelevant. Do not convert every heuristic into
a rigid numerical gate.

## Content fidelity

- [ ] Essential claims and decisions are present.
- [ ] Requirements, prerequisites, constraints, exceptions, and failure modes
      are explicit.
- [ ] Numbers, commands, syntax, links, citations, and formal definitions are
      unchanged or intentionally explained.
- [ ] Removed material was redundant or genuinely out of scope.
- [ ] Uncertain facts are flagged instead of silently rewritten.

## Orientation and priority

- [ ] The title names the subject or reader outcome.
- [ ] The opening states purpose, relevance, scope, or the answer.
- [ ] The primary action or decision is easy to find.
- [ ] The order reflects reader priorities or explains why a different order is
      necessary.
- [ ] Secondary detail has a descriptive place to go.

## Comprehension

- [ ] Headings describe their sections and use a logical hierarchy.
- [ ] Each section has one purpose and an opening that orients the reader.
- [ ] Headings are not stacked without a lead, transition, or meaningful block
      between them.
- [ ] Each paragraph has one dominant topic and an informative opening.
- [ ] Sentences use clear relationships and avoid unnecessary nesting.
- [ ] Jargon, acronyms, and domain terms are defined at the point of need.
- [ ] The document uses one term consistently for one concept.
- [ ] Critical context does not depend on the reader remembering a distant page.

## Visual navigation

- [ ] Lists are used for parallel items or real sequences, not for prose walls.
- [ ] List items are grammatically parallel and scannable.
- [ ] Tables represent comparisons or repeated fields.
- [ ] Code blocks distinguish copyable input, editable values, and expected output.
- [ ] Bold, callouts, and other emphasis are selective and meaningful.
- [ ] Every visually distinct block has a semantic role, such as orientation,
      action, evidence, example, boundary, reference, or exit.
- [ ] Critical warnings and constraints are not available only through visual
      treatment or a collapsed block.
- [ ] Whitespace separates concepts without fragmenting one argument into noise.
- [ ] Links describe their destination or action without surrounding context.

## Action and recovery

- [ ] Procedures state prerequisites before actions.
- [ ] Each meaningful step has a visible outcome or checkpoint.
- [ ] Conditions appear before the action they constrain.
- [ ] Likely errors explain how to recover.
- [ ] The reader can identify the next step after success.

## Accessibility and fit

- [ ] The result works in the requested language and medium.
- [ ] Meaning does not depend on color, motion, icons, typography, or emoji.
- [ ] Informative visuals have useful alternative text where supported.
- [ ] Rich documents preserve semantic headings, contrast, scalable text, and
      user control.
- [ ] The document avoids claims that one format works for every ADHD or
      neurodivergent reader.
- [ ] High-stakes content has a plan for representative user testing.

## Reader test questions

Ask a reviewer to answer these without reading every word:

1. What is this document for?
2. Is it relevant to your task?
3. What is the most important action, decision, or warning?
4. What do you need before starting?
5. Where would you look for an exception or failure mode?
6. What should you do next?

If reviewers cannot answer, revise the information architecture before adding
more emphasis or shortening more sentences.
